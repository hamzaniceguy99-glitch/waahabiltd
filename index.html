How we work

A business support provider on the Isle of Wight

Waahabi. Ltd is a private limited company incorporated in England and Wales on 29 December 2021 with company number 13820571, registered at St John's Place in Newport on the Isle of Wight. Its registered activity is other business support service activities not elsewhere classified. William Blankson has been its director since incorporation. The name is recorded on the register with a full stop after the first word, which is how it appears here.

Business support is an unregulated description and that cuts both ways. It means anybody may offer it, and it also means a client has nothing to check. So the useful thing a provider can publish is where its own edges fall, because the services clients most often ask for sit just beyond them. Forming a company for someone, supplying a registered office or business address, acting as company secretary or providing a nominee makes a firm a trust or company service provider under the money laundering regulations, and that requires registration with HMRC for supervision — a different register from the one every company is already on. Giving immigration advice in the course of a business is a criminal offence unless you are a qualified person, and that catches free help and filling in forms. Holding a client's money can amount to a payment service needing FCA permission. None of those three happen here, and a request for one gets a refusal with the reason.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

Why would a business support firm need to register with HMRC?

Because of what it does rather than what it calls itself. The money laundering regulations define a trust or company service provider by activity: forming companies, firms or other legal persons for other people; providing a registered office, business address or correspondence address for a company; acting, or arranging for someone else to act, as a director, secretary or partner; and acting, or arranging for someone to act, as a nominee shareholder. Carrying on any of those by way of business requires registration with HMRC for anti-money-laundering supervision, and doing it without registration is an offence. The confusion is understandable: people reason that they are a registered company, so they are registered. Those are two unrelated registers.

How would I check whether a provider is supervised?

HMRC publishes a list of the businesses it supervises for anti-money-laundering purposes, and it can be searched. It is worth doing before handing over company formation work or taking a registered office service from someone, for a reason beyond compliance: an unsupervised provider in that space has either not looked into its obligations or has decided to ignore them, and neither says anything encouraging about how it will handle your records. Ask for the registration reference and check it rather than accepting a reassurance.

Can you help with a visa or a sponsor licence?

No, and this is the hardest line in the work. Providing immigration advice or immigration services in the course of a business is a criminal offence under Part V of the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999 unless you are a qualified person — regulated by the Immigration Advice Authority, which was formerly the Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner, or a solicitor, barrister or legal executive regulated by a designated professional body. Two details catch people out. The offence does not depend on charging: free help given in the course of a business is caught. And completing an application form on somebody's behalf is providing an immigration service, not administrative assistance. We will decline and tell you where to look.

What about right to work checks on my own staff?

That is different, and it is worth separating the two clearly. An employer carrying out right to work checks on its own employees is discharging its own duty, not giving immigration advice, and there is nothing to stop us helping you keep those records in order as part of your own administration. What we cannot do is advise an individual about their own immigration position, or about what a check means for their status, or complete anything on their behalf. If a conversation starts drifting that way, we will stop it and say why.

Will you hold money for us, or pay suppliers on our behalf?

No. Receiving, holding, transmitting or paying out money belonging to a client can amount to providing a payment service, which requires authorisation or registration by the Financial Conduct Authority. Rather than operate near that line, we work the other way round: payments stay with you, accounts stay in your name, and we prepare, schedule and reconcile rather than hold. It is a little less convenient in week one and removes a whole category of problem for everybody.

Why list what you cannot do so prominently?

Because the alternative is a client asking for something, getting a vague yes, and both parties finding out later. A description like business support tells a buyer nothing about permissions, so the only informative thing a provider can publish is the boundary. It also saves time: somebody who needs a company formed, a registered office, immigration advice or a payment service can see in ten seconds that this is the wrong door, rather than after two phone calls.
