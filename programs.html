Program 01 · SIC 82990

Back-office administration

In most small businesses the administration is not neglected because it is hard but because it is nobody's actual job, so it gets done at nine in the evening by whoever feels guiltiest. Handing it over works when the handover is specific: which tasks, on what day, with what access, and what gets escalated rather than decided. Vague help is what produces the second mess.

Program 02 · Your company, your records

Company records and filings

Two things cause almost all the avoidable trouble here: a deadline nobody owned, and registers that were never written up after a change. Both are cheap to fix and expensive to ignore, since late filing carries penalties and an unexplained gap on the public record is read by anyone checking you out. Note the limit, though: this is help with your own company's records, which is a different thing from forming companies for other people.

  • Per company
  • Owner-managed companies
  • Deadline calendar

Program 03 · Once, properly

Processes written down

Every small business has three or four processes that exist entirely inside one person's head, and the cost of that only becomes visible in the week they are unavailable. Writing them down is dull, takes an afternoon each, and is the single highest-return piece of administration available to a small company. The test is whether a competent stranger could follow it without ringing anybody.

  • Small businesses
  • Written and tested

Program 04 · What you forgot you pay

Costs and subscriptions

Nearly every business that has traded for a few years is paying for at least one thing nobody uses, one thing bought twice, and one contract that renewed automatically because the notice period passed unnoticed. Finding them is a morning's work with a bank statement. The part people get wrong is cancelling: a cancellation that is not confirmed in writing is a cancellation that may not have happened.
